## Mathematical self-check

**Literal answer: false.** If a proposed witness is x, the allowed case y = x requires age(x) > age(x), which no ordinary numerical age satisfies. If H were empty, the existential would still be false.

**Different verification route: negate the claim.** Its negation is $\forall x\in H\;\exists y\in H,\;\operatorname{age}(x)\leq\operatorname{age}(y)$. Every x has the witness y = x by reflexivity of ≤, so the negation is true; for empty H it is vacuously true. Both routes agree. This is an author self-check, not independent peer review.

## Competing readings and authoring choices

| Reading | Truth status | Authoring decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Literal strict comparison with every human | False for every domain H, including empty H | Keep this reading for the supplied formula; reject the proposed true key. |
| Strictly older than every *other* human: $\exists x\in H\;\forall y\in H,\;(y\ne x\Rightarrow\operatorname{age}(x)>\operatorname{age}(y))$ | True exactly when a unique person attains the greatest age; false for an empty domain, a tie at the greatest age, or no attained maximum | Conditional, not established over unspecified humans. Excluding self alone cannot justify true. |
| At least as old as every human: $\exists x\in H\;\forall y\in H,\;\operatorname{age}(x)\geq\operatorname{age}(y)$ | True on a finite nonempty domain of numerical ages, including ties; false on an empty domain and potentially false on an infinite domain with no attained maximum | Viable only as an explicitly changed, original statement with sufficient assumptions. |

The two repaired readings are possibilities, not claims about the missing PDF. An infinite domain may have a maximum or lack one; boundedness alone also does not ensure attainment. On a singleton domain, the literal statement is false while both repaired readings are true (the “other human” condition is vacuous). Two people tied for greatest age make the strict-other reading false and the non-strict reading true.

Review decision: blocking rather than minor, because the supplied true key would grade correct reasoning as wrong. The literal reading passes correctness and unambiguity with key false; neither repaired reading may inherit a true key without its own stated assumptions. For authoring, retain the literal false claim to target self-comparison, or prefer a small fictional finite domain with explicit numerical ages. Resolve lesson alignment first. Do not use real-world uniqueness of an oldest human as an unstated premise.
