## Correction design decisions

The competing readings of the old “When?” prompt are a characterization (a correct) and any sufficient situation (d also true). This is a blocking single-choice ambiguity, not an incorrect answer key.

Candidates scored on correctness/domain/unambiguity gates and quality (maximum 12):

- Keep the old stem: rejected at the unambiguity gate because both tautologies suffices.
- Explicitly ask for a necessary and sufficient condition: gates pass; quality 12. Selected because it preserves O04 and all existing corrective feedback.
- Change to multiple-choice accepting a and d: rejected at the checker type gate; would change the grading contract and objective demand.

Misconception alternatives: sufficient versus necessary (12, selected), agreement on some rows (11, retained in option b), row value versus relation (9, already covered by E03/E06). Existing misconception tags retained after listing repository tags.

Distractor alternatives: retain existing options (12, selected); replace tautologies with contradictions (10, duplicates polarity confusion); remove option d (8, loses the exact misconception the correction addresses). The remaining answer is a. The inherited bank always places correct choices first; validator reports this warning. Reordering unrelated items is outside this focused correction.

## Answer-key verification

Author self-check with a different derivation; not independent peer review.

- E01 (a): the definition is the conjunction of the two implications. Enumeration separately confirms equivalence of the definition and biconditional.
- E02: hand application of the two implications gives true, false, false, true in row order 00, 01, 10, 11. The helper verifies all four stored cells.
- E03 (a): a connective builds an assertion evaluated per valuation; semantic equivalence compares functions over all valuations. A row-dependent assertion and equality of entire truth functions are distinct types of objects, so swapping their roles cannot be correct. This is a terminology check, not a helper-verified numerical key.
- E04 (a): by definition, equivalence means agreement at every valuation; the biconditional is true exactly at agreeing valuations. Independently, expanding into both implications shows that neither direction has a counterexample exactly when the biconditional is a tautology. Both tautologies forces agreement and is sufficient, but commutativity of conjunction supplies equivalent contingent assertions, so it is not necessary. The helper separately confirms commutativity, contingency, the both-tautologies example and the non-equivalence of conjunction and disjunction. It does not directly verify natural-language necessity/sufficiency.
- E05: De Morgan’s law makes the two operands identical in value. Independent full enumeration verifies all four true cells.
- E06 (a): equivalence compares whole tables and cannot take a value on an individual row. Separately, the actual biconditional at A=true, B=false is false, matching option b rather than a. Options c and d restate the whole-table criterion and definition. The helper verifies the row calculation only.

Edge cases include equal false values, unequal values, equivalent contingent assertions and both tautologies. Only E04’s stem changes; a remains the unique necessary-and-sufficient choice. FR “condition nécessaire et suffisante” and EN “necessary and sufficient condition” have matching meaning and identical mathematical strings. Existing glossary terminology for semantic equivalence and biconditional is preserved.
