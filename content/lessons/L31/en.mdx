# Half-planes and triangles

An equation selects a boundary; an inequality selects one side. Three well-chosen constraints can then fill a triangle. Revisit membership testing ([L28](../L28/)) and intersections ([L30](../L30/)); also recall that multiplication by a negative number reverses an inequality.

## Objectives and prerequisites

- Test a half-plane inequality.
- Distinguish included and excluded boundaries.
- Choose a side using a test point.
- Combine several constraints.
- Test membership in a triangle.
- Analyse sign and degeneracy.

Prerequisites: L24, L28, L30. Domains and restrictions stated below are part of every rule.

## 1. Understand — One boundary, two regions

Imagine a map where a straight line separates two zones. To decide whether a point is on the correct side, we can look at the map or perform an exact calculation.

In L28, equation $y=2x+1$ selected only the points on the line. Now an inequality selects all points on one side: $y>2x+1$ means the point's height exceeds the line's height at the same horizontal coordinate.

**PDF, p. 50.** Line $ax+by=c$, with $(a,b)\ne(0,0)$, divides the plane into two open half-planes: $ax+by>c$ and $ax+by<c$. The line itself belongs to neither. Closed versions include their boundary.

Our mission: decide which side contains $A=(0,3)$ and $B=(2,0)$ relative to the PDF line. We also add $D=(0,1)$, on the boundary.

### Three half-planes, one triangle

**Project supplement, outside the revised PDF.** We want to fill the triangle with vertices $A=(0,0)$, $B=(4,0)$ and $C=(0,3)$. Each edge imposes a rule. First keep the right side of the vertical axis, then the area above the horizontal axis, then the area below BC.

## 3. Calculate — One condition at a time

### The points from the PDF

Let $f(x,y)=y-2x-1$. This means calculating the point's height minus the line's height.

| Point | Point height | Line height | Difference | Conclusion |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $A=(0,3)$ | 3 | $2\cdot0+1=1$ | $3-1=2$ | Above |
| $B=(2,0)$ | 0 | $2\cdot2+1=5$ | $0-5=-5$ | Below |
| $D=(0,1)$ | 1 | $2\cdot0+1=1$ | $1-1=0$ | On the line |

In general, for $f(x,y)=ax+by-c$, with $(a,b)\ne(0,0)$, the boundary is $f=0$. The sign of f determines the half-plane.

If two points give opposite signs, f passes through zero on the segment joining them: the segment crosses the line. If the values have the same strict sign, their weighted averages retain that sign. The segment stays on the same side.

### Choose the correct side: a test point

To draw $x+2y\le6$:

1. Draw boundary $x+2y=6$, for example by joining $(0,3)$ and $(6,0)$.
2. Choose a point off the boundary: here the origin.
3. Substitute: $0+2\cdot0=0\le6$. The origin is accepted.
4. Shade the side containing the origin; draw the boundary solid.

Point $(4,2)$ gives $4+2\cdot2=8>6$: it is excluded. Point $(2,2)$ gives $2+2\cdot2=6$: included for the inclusive inequality but excluded for the strict inequality. A boundary point cannot select a side.

### Build the three triangle constraints

Edges AB and AC are the axes. For AB, keep $y\ge0$ because C is above; for AC, keep $x\ge0$ because B is to the right.

Edge BC passes through $(4,0)$ and $(0,3)$. Equation $3x+4y=12$ is true at both vertices: $3\cdot4+4\cdot0=12$ and $3\cdot0+4\cdot3=12$. The origin, the opposite vertex, gives $0\le12$; hence retain $3x+4y\le12$.

All three rules must be true simultaneously:

$$x\ge0,\qquad y\ge0,\qquad 3x+4y\le12.$$

| Test | $P=(1,1)$ | $Q=(3,2)$ |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $x\ge0$ | $1\ge0$: yes | $3\ge0$: yes |
| $y\ge0$ | $1\ge0$: yes | $2\ge0$: yes |
| $3x+4y\le12$ | $3+4=7\le12$: yes | $9+8=17>12$: no |
| Conclusion | Inside | Outside |

Point $(2,3/2)$ is on BC because $3\cdot2+4\cdot(3/2)=12$: the boundary is included. Another example: $x\ge0$, $y\ge0$, $x+y\le4$ describes the closed triangle with vertices $(0,0)$, $(4,0)$ and $(0,4)$.

### General triangle: compare signs

For each edge of a nondegenerate triangle, take the opposite vertex as reference R. It is off the line, so $f(R)\ne0$. We want to know whether P lies on the same side as R.

| Sign of f(P) | Sign of f(R) | Product | Decision |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| + | + | + | Same side |
| − | − | + | Same side |
| + or − | Opposite | − | Opposite side |
| 0 | Nonzero | 0 | On the boundary |

The closed test is $f(P)f(R)\ge0$; the strict test is $f(P)f(R)>0$.

Example for BC: choose $f(x,y)=3x+4y-12$. At vertex A, $f(A)=-12$. For P, $f(P)=-5$; the product is $(-5)(-12)=60>0$. For Q, $f(Q)=5$; the product is $5(-12)=-60<0$. Repeat the test for the other two edges.

Multiplying f by a negative number reverses both signs but not the same-side decision. In contrast, multiplying an inequality by a negative number requires reversing its direction. Aligned or repeated vertices give zero area: filling must stop because there is no triangle with nonempty interior.

## 5. Practise — From sign to reasoning

**Easy.** Does point $(1,3)$ satisfy $y>2x+1$? What about $y\ge2x+1$?

<details>
<summary>Check the answer</summary>

The line's height is $2\cdot1+1=3$. The point is on the boundary: the strict inequality is false and the inclusive inequality is true.

</details>

**Intermediate.** Does point $(3,2)$ belong to the triangle with vertices $(0,0),(4,0),(0,3)$?

<details>
<summary>Check the answer</summary>

Constraints $x\ge0$ and $y\ge0$ are true, but $3\cdot3+4\cdot2=17>12$. The point is excluded: passing two of three tests is not sufficient.

</details>

**Challenge.** The boundary is $x-y=0$. Point $R=(2,0)$ is the reference. Is point $P=(1,3)$ on the same side as R? What if we use $y-x=0$?

<details>
<summary>Show the worked solution</summary>

With $f(x,y)=x-y$, we get $f(R)=2$ and $f(P)=-2$, so the product is $-4<0$. The points lie on opposite sides. Reversing f reverses the signs but leaves the product unchanged: the decision does not change.

</details>

## 6. Remember — Printable revision sheet

- Boundary: $f(x,y)=ax+by-c=0$, with $(a,b)\ne(0,0)$.
- Positive, negative or zero sign: two sides or boundary.
- Excluded boundary: $<$ or $>$; included boundary: $\le$ or $\ge$.
- Test point: choose a point off the line, substitute, retain the correct side.
- Same closed side: $f(P)f(R)\ge0$ if $f(R)\ne0$.
- Closed triangle: retain the half-plane of the opposite vertex for each of the three edges; all tests must pass.
- Traps: test point on the boundary, reversed sign after negative multiplication, confusing “and” with “or”, aligned or repeated vertices.
