# What does a wrong model cost?

You prepare a code for announced probabilities, but symbols follow another distribution. Gibbs' lemma explains why average surprise calculated with the wrong model cannot beat actual entropy. Before coding, we need a small calculus tool: a logarithm inequality.

**Source: PDF, pp. 46–48.** We expand every proof step, using base-2 logarithms to measure bits.

## Objectives and prerequisites

You will: (1) prove the logarithm inequality; (2) identify equality; (3) validate two positive distributions; (4) prove Gibbs; (5) calculate the model gap; (6) distinguish ideal and integer lengths. Prerequisites: entropy [L48](/en/lessons/L48/), the logarithm derivative and derivative signs.

## 01 · A curve below its tangent

For $x>0$, set $g(x)=x-1-\ln x$. Its derivative is

$$g'(x)=1-\frac1x=\frac{x-1}{x}.$$

The denominator is positive: the derivative is negative before 1 and positive after 1. The function therefore reaches its global minimum at 1, where $g(1)=0$. Thus

$$\ln x\le x-1\qquad(x>0),$$

with equality **if and only if** $x=1$. Geometrically, the logarithm curve stays below its tangent at 1. Do not directly replace the natural logarithm by base 2: divide by $\ln2>0$.

## 02 · State Gibbs with all assumptions

Take two lists of the same size $p_i>0$, $q_i>0$, with $\sum_i p_i=\sum_i q_i=1$. The lemma states

$$-\sum_i p_i\log_2q_i\ge-\sum_i p_i\log_2p_i=H(p).$$

On the left, model-q surprise is averaged using the **actual weights p**. This quantity is also called cross entropy. Equality holds exactly when $p_i=q_i$ for every i. We remain in the strictly positive setting; no logarithm of zero is used.

## 03 · Four proof steps

1. The ratio is positive, so applying the inequality to $x=q_i/p_i$ gives

$$\ln(q_i/p_i)\le q_i/p_i-1.$$

2. Multiply by $p_i>0$: the inequality keeps its direction. Then sum:

$$\sum_i p_i\ln(q_i/p_i)\le\sum_i(q_i-p_i)=1-1=0.$$

3. Expand the quotient logarithm and change sign. The direction reverses:

$$-\sum_i p_i\ln q_i\ge-\sum_i p_i\ln p_i.$$

4. Divide by $\ln2>0$ to obtain base-2 logarithms and the stated lemma.

**Equality.** Every logarithm-inequality gap is nonnegative and weighted by a strictly positive number. Their sum can be zero only if every gap is zero: each ratio equals 1. This gives precisely $p_i=q_i$ for every i. Equality of the probability sums alone is insufficient.

## 04 · A worked example

For $p=(1/2,1/2)$, entropy is 1 bit. Choose $q=(1/4,3/4)$:

$$-\sum_i p_i\log_2q_i=\frac12\cdot2+\frac12\log_2(4/3)\approx1{,}2075.$$

The ideal overhead is about 0.2075 bits per symbol. If q becomes p, cross entropy becomes 1 and the gap vanishes. **Careful:** these logarithms are not necessarily integer lengths achievable symbol by symbol. Gibbs prepares a bound; it does not directly construct a code.

## Exercises and solutions

1. Why is the inequality's domain strictly positive?
2. For $p=(1/2,1/4,1/4)$ and $q=(1/3,1/3,1/3)$, calculate both sides of Gibbs.
3. If both distributions sum to 1, why is equality not automatic?

<details className="info-answer">
<summary>Justified solutions</summary>

1. The logarithm and proof ratio must be defined. This proof does not handle zeros through an unstated convention.
2. Entropy is $3/2$; cross entropy is $\log_2 3\approx1{,}585$. The inequality is strict because p and q differ.
3. The sum of differences vanishes using normalisation, but equality in each logarithm application additionally requires every ratio to equal 1.

</details>
