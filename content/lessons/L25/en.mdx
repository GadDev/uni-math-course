# Systems of two equations

One purchase may leave several possible prices. A second purchase supplies another piece of information: both must hold together. A system organises this search and explains why zero, one or infinitely many pairs remain.

## Objectives and prerequisites

- Read a solution pair.
- Substitute an unknown.
- Eliminate an unknown.
- Translate a problem.
- Distinguish zero, one and infinitely many solutions.
- Analyse a parameterised system.

Prerequisites: L24. Domains and restrictions stated below are part of every rule.

**Reminder from [L24](../L24/).** A reversible transformation preserves the solution set. Symbol $\mathbb R^2$ denotes pairs of real numbers; $(x;y)$ has a fixed order. Here a **row** of the system is an equation, not a calculation step. A **parameter** is fixed during solving; its possible values are then compared.

## 1. Understand — Satisfy two pieces of information together

The professor poses this problem on PDF pages 23–24: a coffee and a croissant cost 4.50 euros; two coffees and a croissant cost 7 euros. What does a coffee cost?

Let c be the coffee price and k the croissant price, in euros. Prices are nonnegative. Translate the purchases:

$$\begin{cases}c+k=4{,}5 &(L_1)\\2c+k=7 &(L_2)\end{cases}$$

One equation is insufficient: a coffee at 1.50 euros and croissant at 3 euros, or a coffee at 2.50 euros and croissant at 2 euros satisfy the first purchase. Check the second too. The brace means AND; a solution pair satisfies both rows in the same order.

## 3. Calculate — Two methods, the same problem

**A — Elimination and substitution side by side.**

| Elimination | Substitution |
| --- | --- |
| Retain the first row; subtract it from the second. | Isolate k in the first row. |
| $(2c+k)-(c+k)=7-4{,}5$ | $k=4{,}5-c$ |
| $2c+k-c-k=2{,}5$ | $2c+(4{,}5-c)=7$ |
| $c=2{,}5$ | $c=7-4{,}5=2{,}5$ |
| $k=4{,}5-2{,}5=2$ | $k=4{,}5-2{,}5=2$ |

In elimination, the k terms cancel; in substitution, the entire expression in parentheses replaces k. The operations remain reversible if the first condition is retained. Both methods give:

$$S=\{(2{,}5;2)\}.$$

Coffee costs 2.50 euros and the croissant 2 euros. Check both purchases and context: $2{,}5+2=4{,}5$ and $2\cdot2{,}5+2=7$. Swapping the pair changes which price is which.

**B — Choose a method and align coefficients.**

If an unknown is isolated or easy to isolate, try substitution. If two coefficients are identical or opposite, subtract or add. Otherwise multiply an entire row by a nonzero number to align coefficients. Never multiply a single term alone.

Additional example:

$$\begin{cases}2x+3y=13\\3x+2y=12\end{cases}$$

Multiply the first row by 2 and the second by 3: $4x+6y=26$, $9x+6y=36$. Subtract the first new row from the second: $5x=10$, $x=2$. Return to an original equation: $4+3y=13$, $y=3$. Check: $2\cdot2+3\cdot3=13$, $3\cdot2+2\cdot3=12$.

**Guided example: substitution**

For $x+y=9$ and $2x-y=3$, the first row gives $y=9-x$. Substitution in the second gives $2x-(9-x)=3$, so $3x=12$. This yields $x=4$, then $y=5$. Pair $(4,5)$ satisfies both rows. The steps are reversible, so it is the unique solution.

**Guided example: elimination and units**

Two adult tickets and one child ticket cost $19$ euros; an adult and a child cost $12$ euros. Let $a$ and $e$ be prices in euros; the equations are $2a+e=19$ and $a+e=12$. Subtracting the second from the first gives $a=7$, then $e=5$. Reconstructing both purchases checks the model as well as the arithmetic.

**C — Interpret the elimination result.**

| System | After elimination | Solution set |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $x+y=3$, $x-y=1$ | $2y=2$ | $S=\{(2;1)\}$ |
| $x+y=2$, $2x+2y=5$ | $0=1$ | $S=\varnothing$ |
| $x+y=6$, $2x+2y=12$ | $0=0$ | $S=\{(t;6-t):t\in\mathbb R\}$ |

In the first case, keep one equation to obtain x. In the last, one equation remains: freely choose t then calculate y. If both equations reduce to identities with no unknowns, every real pair works; a contradiction always rules out every pair. The three diagrams assume each equation defines a line.

**D — Parameters and constraints.**

In $x+y=5$ and $x+ay=5$, subtracting the rows gives $(a-1)y=0$. Handle the coefficient before dividing:

- $a\ne1$ : $y=0$, $x=5$, $S=\{(5;0)\}$.
- $a=1$ : $S=\{(t;5-t):t\in\mathbb R\}$.

Dividing by a−1 without a restriction would lose an entire family of solutions. After the calculation, check context restrictions, including units and price signs.

## 5. Practise — Solve, count, justify

### 1. Apply

Solve $x+y=7$ and $x-y=1$.

Adding the rows eliminates one unknown.

<details>
<summary>Explained solution</summary>

We obtain $2x=8$, then $x=4$ and $y=3$. Check: $4+3=7$ and $4-3=1$.

### 2. Combine

Count solutions of $x+y=2$ and $2x+2y=5$.

<details>
<summary>Hint</summary>

Subtract twice the first row from the second.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Explained solution</summary>

The calculation gives $0=1$, which is impossible. The solution set is empty; no pair satisfies both rows.

</details>

</div>


<div className="algebra-exercise">

### 3. Reason

Solve $x+y=2$ and $x+ay=2$ according to real parameter $a$.

<details>
<summary>Hint</summary>

The coefficient to examine is $a-1$.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Explained solution</summary>

Subtraction gives $(a-1)y=0$. If $a\ne1$, then $y=0$ and $x=2$. If $a=1$, all pairs $(t;2-t)$ with $t\in\mathbb R$ satisfy the system. Letter $t$ describes the free coordinate.

</details>

## 6. Remember — Printable revision sheet

<div className="algebra-revision">

- Define both unknowns, units and domain. Translate the two simultaneous conditions.
- Substitution: isolate, replace the whole group, solve, recover the second unknown.
- Elimination: align coefficients by multiplying entire rows by nonzero constants; add or subtract, retain an equation.
- Contradiction: empty set. Identity: examine the remaining condition and describe the family. Handle zero coefficients before dividing.
- Check both original equations and constraints; respect pair order.

**Self-check.** Solve the first exercise using both methods, explain the three outcomes, then handle the parameter in the third without premature division.
