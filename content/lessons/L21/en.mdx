# Calculating with real numbers

A calculation resembles a recipe: changing the order of the steps can change the result. This lesson teaches you to read the instruction before calculating. It prepares [L22 — expanding and factorising](../L22/), then equations in L24. The added activities are original project material.

## Objectives and prerequisites

- Respect precedence.
- Distinguish sign from exponentiation.
- Calculate with negative numbers.
- Use operation properties.
- Identify undefined operations.
- Analyse a transformation.

Prerequisites: L10. Domains and restrictions stated below are part of every rule.

## Order of operations: one calculation, one step

**PDF example, pages 4–5 — rewritten explanation.** In $2+3\cdot4^2$, the square applies to $4$, not to $3\cdot4$.

1. Power: $4^2=16$.
2. Multiplication: $3\cdot16=48$.
3. Addition: $2+48=50$.

Read this as “two plus three times the square of four”. In contrast, $(2+3)\cdot4^2=80$: parentheses change the instruction. For $8\div2(2+2)$, this course’s stated convention gives multiplication and division equal priority, working left to right: $8\div2\cdot4=16$. Write $8/(2(2+2))$ if you intend $1$; make the grouping visible.

**Additional example.** $-3^2+(-3)^2=-9+9=0$. The first term takes the opposite of a square; the second squares the negative number itself. A power’s base is the number or group receiving the exponent.

## The calculation contract

Work in $\mathbb{R}$. An expression is a calculation instruction, not simply a sequence to read from left to right. Parentheses, powers, products and quotients, then sums and differences determine the order. At equal precedence, process products and quotients from left to right, as with sums and differences. Write the grouping: a fraction bar groups its whole numerator and denominator.

## Basic rules: opposite and reciprocal

The opposite of $a$ is $-a$: together they sum to zero, $a+(-a)=0$. For $a\ne0$, its reciprocal is $1/a$: their product is one, $a(1/a)=1$. Thus the opposite of $4$ is $-4$, whereas its reciprocal is $1/4$. Subtraction and division use these different ideas: $a-b=a+(-b)$ and $a/b=a(1/b)$ for $b\ne0$.

The numbers $0$ and $1$ have specific roles: $a+0=a$, $a\cdot1=a$ and $a\cdot0=0$. A minus before a group multiplies the **whole** group by $-1$: $-(a+b)=-a-b$ and $a-(b-c)=a-b+c$. For example, $7-(2-5)=7-2+5=10$. Calculating $2-5=-3$ first gives the same result.

## Signs and operations

Subtracting $b$ means adding $-b$. Thus $a-b=a+(-b)$ and $a-(-b)=a+b$. A minus sign before a power does not automatically belong to its base: $-4^2=-16$, whereas $(-4)^2=16$. A product of two negative numbers is positive; this does not make a sum of two negative numbers positive.

## Understand: sign, base and exponent

For a power, first ask: **which number is multiplied by itself?** If $a\ge0$ and $n$ is a positive integer, $-a^n=-(a^n)$ means calculate the power of $a$, then its opposite. In contrast, $(-a)^n$ multiplies the number $-a$ by itself $n$ times.

For $a=3$ and $n=2$: $-3^2=-(3\cdot3)=-9$, but $(-3)^2=(-3)(-3)=9$. For $n=3$, both expressions equal $-27$: this agreement does not make the instructions identical. Each pair of negative factors has positive product; with an odd count, one minus sign remains.

## Guided example: preserve the structure

Calculate $18-2(3+1)^2/4$. First $3+1=4$, then $4^2=16$. The product and quotient give $2\cdot16/4=8$. Finally $18-8=10$. Check using another form: $2/4=1/2$, so subtract half of $16$. This regrouping concerns products here, not an addition that could be moved arbitrarily.

## What changes and what stays

Addition and multiplication are **commutative** (swap the two numbers: $a+b=b+a$, $ab=ba$) and **associative** (change the groups: $(a+b)+c=a+(b+c)$, $(ab)c=a(bc)$). The values remain unchanged. Subtraction and division are not. For example, $12/(3/2)=8$, but $(12/3)/2=2$. Distributivity connects the two operations: $a(b+c)=ab+ac$. Changing a form is useful only when its value and domain are preserved.

## Guided example: zero is not permission

For $a\ne0$, $a/a=1$. At $a=0$, this expression is undefined. The equality $0\cdot u=0$ holds for every real $u$: it therefore cannot determine a quotient $0/0$. Likewise, $0u=0v$ does not imply $u=v$. Before cancelling, name the factor you divide by and check that it is nonzero.

## How to: a reusable method

1. Identify groups and the base of each power.
2. Evaluate parentheses, powers, products and quotients, then sums and differences. At equal priority, work left to right.
3. Copy unchanged parts: each line must remain equal to the preceding line.
4. Before dividing, check that the denominator is nonzero. Check the result with another permitted grouping.

**Spot the mistake.** “Two negatives make a positive” concerns a product or subtraction of a negative: $(-2)+(-3)=-5$, whereas $(-2)(-3)=6$.

## Essentials and self-check

- Read an expression by grouping and precedence, not only left to right.
- The opposite undoes addition; the reciprocal undoes multiplication and requires a nonzero number.
- A sign before a group affects the entire group.
