# Calculating with real numbers

A calculation resembles a recipe: changing the order of the steps can change the result. This lesson teaches you to read the instruction before calculating. It prepares [L22 — expanding and factorising](../L22/), then equations in L24. The added activities are original project material.

## Objectives and prerequisites

- Respect precedence.
- Distinguish sign from exponentiation.
- Calculate with negative numbers.
- Use operation properties.
- Identify undefined operations.
- Analyse a transformation.

Prerequisites: L10. Domains and restrictions stated below are part of every rule.

**Useful reminder.** $\mathbb R$ denotes the real numbers: integers, fractions and numbers such as $\sqrt2$. Symbol $\in$ means “belongs to”. Equality compares two values; a minus sign may mark a negative number or subtraction. Review [number families in L10](../L10/) if needed.

## 1. Understand — Read an instruction before calculating

Imagine two purchases: an item costing 2 euros and three items whose price is the square of 4 (16 euros each in this fictional example). Expression $2+3\cdot4^2$ describes the total. Reading left to right is not enough: identify which operation applies to which group. Pages 4–5 of the PDF give this numerical example.

A minus sign can have three roles: subtraction between numbers, the opposite of a number or group, and the sign of a negative number. Before calculating, identify its role.

## 3. Calculate — One operation at a time

### A. Precedence from the PDF, pages 4–5

| Expression | Operation performed |
| --- | --- |
| $2+3\cdot4^2$ | Start: identify base 4. |
| $2+3\cdot16$ | Power. |
| $2+48$ | Multiplication. |
| $50$ | Addition. |

Copy the unchanged parts on each line. With parentheses, $(2+3)\cdot4^2=5\cdot16=80$. Another example: $18-2(3+1)^2/4=18-2\cdot16/4=18-8=10$.

At equal priority, multiplication and division proceed left to right, as do addition and subtraction. The PDF resolves its example $8\div2(2+2)=8\div2\cdot4=16$ using this convention. To avoid ambiguous implicit multiplication, write $(8/2)(2+2)$ or $8/(2(2+2))=1$ as intended. A fraction bar groups its entire numerator and denominator.

### B. Opposite, reciprocal and identities (PDF, page 6)

| Concept | Example for 4 | Check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Opposite | $-4$ | $4+(-4)=0$ |
| Reciprocal | $1/4$ | $4\cdot(1/4)=1$ |

The opposite of $a$ is $-a$ ; it exists even for zero. The reciprocal is $1/a$ only if $a\ne0$. Subtracting means adding the opposite; dividing means multiplying by the reciprocal:

$$a-b=a+(-b),\qquad a/b=a(1/b)\quad(b\ne0).$$

Zero is the additive identity, one the multiplicative identity and zero annihilates a product: $a+0=a$, $a\cdot1=a$, $a\cdot0=0$.

### C. Why do signs work?

Be careful with the rule “two negatives make a positive”: the operation matters.

| Operation | Calculation |
| --- | --- |
| Adding two negatives | $(-2)+(-3)=-5$ |
| Subtracting a negative | $2-(-3)=2+3=5$ |
| Product of two negatives | $(-2)(-3)=6$ |

The PDF states the general rule $(-a)(-b)=ab$. Here is an original explanation using distributivity:

$$0=(-3)(2+(-2))$$

$$0=(-3)\cdot2+(-3)(-2)=-6+(-3)(-2)$$

$$6=(-3)(-2).$$

The required product must cancel −6. Signs are not erased by convention: the result follows from arithmetic rules. In particular, a minus before a group multiplies every term by −1: $-(a+b)=-a-b$, $a-(b-c)=a-b+c$.

Example: $7-(2-5)=7-2+5=10$. We obtain the same result by first calculating $2-5=-3$.

### D. Sign, base and exponent

| Expression | Factors and sign | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $-3^2$ | $-(3\cdot3)$ | $-9$ |
| $(-3)^2$ | $(-3)(-3)$ | $9$ |
| $-3^3$ | $-(3\cdot3\cdot3)$ | $-27$ |
| $(-3)^3$ | $(-3)(-3)(-3)$ | $-27$ |

For strictly positive a, an even power of −a is positive and an odd power is negative. If a is zero, both expressions are zero for every positive integer exponent. Agreement in the odd case does not let us remove parentheses in general.

### E. Change the expression without changing its value

The PDF, page 6, gives three properties. For all real numbers:

| Property | Rule | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Commutativity: swap | $a+b=b+a$, $ab=ba$ | $2+3=3+2$ |
| Associativity: regroup | $(a+b)+c=a+(b+c)$, $(ab)c=a(bc)$ | $(2+3)+4=2+(3+4)$ |
| Distributivity: multiply each term | $a(b+c)=ab+ac$ | $2(3+4)=6+8$ |

Subtraction and division are generally neither commutative nor associative. For example $12/(3/2)=8$, but $(12/3)/2=2$. Do not change groups arbitrarily.

### F. Why can't we divide by zero?

Division finds the number that, multiplied by the divisor, gives the dividend. For $6/0$, no real u satisfies $0u=6$. For $0/0$, every real u satisfies $0u=0$. In both cases there is no unique quotient. Thus $a/a=1$ only if $a\ne0$. We cannot conclude $u=v$ from $0u=0v$.

## 5. Practise — From calculation to reasoning

### 1. Apply

Calculate $6-2\cdot3^2$.

<details>
<summary>Hint</summary>

What is the base of the square?

</details>

<details>
<summary>Explained solution</summary>

The square gives $3^2=9$, then $2\cdot9=18$, so $6-18=-12$.

</details>

### 2. Combine

Compare $-2^4$ and $(-2)^4$.

<details>
<summary>Hint</summary>

Is the minus sign inside the base?

</details>

<details>
<summary>Explained solution</summary>

$-2^4=-(2^4)=-16$; $(-2)^4=16$. An even power of a real number is nonnegative; its opposite is negative here.

</details>

</div>

### 3. Reason

Can we conclude $u=v$ from $0u=0v$? Justify.

<details>
<summary>Hint</summary>

Look for two distinct numbers satisfying the equality.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Explained solution</summary>

No: $u=1$ and $v=2$ give $0=0$ without being equal. Division by zero is undefined over the reals.

## 6. Remember — Printable revision sheet

<div className="algebra-revision">

- Precedence: groups, powers, products/quotients, sums/differences; at the last two levels, left to right.
- Opposite: sum equals zero; reciprocal: product equals one, for a nonzero number.
- $a-(-b)=a+b$ ; $(-a)(-b)=ab$ ; $-(a+b)=-a-b$.
- Identify the base: $-3^2=-9$, $(-3)^2=9$.
- Addition and multiplication: commutativity, associativity; distributivity: every term receives the factor.
- Never divide by zero. Check the domain and copy each unchanged part.
