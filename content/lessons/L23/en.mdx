# Fractions, powers and roots

Simplifying makes a form lighter without changing its meaning. Before using the “calculator machine”, check whether the input is allowed. We separate three actions: adding shares, repeating factors and finding a root.

## Objectives and prerequisites

- Add fractions.
- Multiply and divide.
- Use powers.
- Calculate a real root.
- Preserve the domain.
- Analyse a simplification.

Prerequisites: L21, L22. Domains and restrictions stated below are part of every rule.

**Reminder from [L21](../L21/) and [L22](../L22/).** A factor is multiplied; a term is added. You may cancel $2x/(2y)$ to $x/y$ when $y\ne0$; you cannot remove the $2$ in $(x+2)/(y+2)$. The **domain** is the set of values for which every calculation is defined.

## 3. Calculate — Three modules, one operation at a time

<span id="module-fractions"></span>

**A — Fractions (PDF, page 13).**

In fraction $a/b$, the denominator must be nonzero: $b\ne0$. Write the PDF example one line at a time:

$$\frac12+\frac13=\frac{1\cdot3}{2\cdot3}+\frac{1\cdot2}{3\cdot2}=\frac36+\frac26=\frac56.$$

Why multiply top and bottom? Because multiplying by $3/3=1$ does not change the value. Result $2/5$ is wrong: it mixes share sizes.

A fraction represents division. The denominator gives the size of the shares; the numerator counts those shares. For $b,d\ne0$, the rules are:

<div className="method-table" tabIndex={0} role="region" aria-label="Four-operation reminder; scrollable table">

| Operation | Rule | Example with steps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Addition | $\frac ab+\frac cd=\frac{ad+bc}{bd}$ | $\frac12+\frac13=\frac36+\frac26=\frac56$ |
| Subtraction | $\frac ab-\frac cd=\frac{ad-bc}{bd}$ | $\frac34-\frac16=\frac9{12}-\frac2{12}=\frac7{12}$ |
| Multiplication | $\frac ab\cdot\frac cd=\frac{ac}{bd}$ | $\frac23\cdot\frac35=\frac6{15}=\frac25$ |
| Division, also requiring $c\ne0$ | $\frac ab\div\frac cd=\frac ab\cdot\frac dc=\frac{ad}{bc}$ | $\frac34\div\frac98=\frac34\cdot\frac89=\frac{24}{36}=\frac23$ |

</div>

**To add or subtract**, make the shares equally sized, then add or subtract their counts. The common denominator may be the product of the denominators; a smaller common multiple also works. Never add fractions by adding their denominators.

**To multiply**, no common denominator is needed. **To divide**, keep the first fraction and invert only the nonzero second one. A reciprocal “undoes” multiplication because $\frac cd\cdot\frac dc=1$ when $c,d\ne0$.

**Finally, reduce** by dividing numerator and denominator by the same nonzero integer: $\frac6{15}=\frac{6\div3}{15\div3}=\frac25$. A denominator sign may move to the numerator: $\frac{a}{-b}=-\frac ab$. A zero fraction is an allowed number; using it as a divisor, or a zero denominator, makes the calculation undefined.

Check a division by recovering the dividend: $(2/3)(9/8)=3/4$. This explains why only the divisor is inverted.

Another rule from the PDF, page 13: $\frac{ac}{bc}=\frac ab$ if $b,c\ne0$. Cancel common factors, never terms of a sum. Compare $\frac{2x}{2y}=\frac xy$ for $y\ne0$ with $\frac{x+2}{y+2}$, where the 2s are not common factors.

**B — Powers (PDF, page 14).**

A positive integer exponent counts factors: $a^3=a\cdot a\cdot a$. To understand the two new rules, start with a nonzero base:

$$2^0=\frac{2^3}{2^3}=1,\qquad 2^{-3}=\frac{1}{2^3}=\frac18.$$

A negative exponent means reciprocal. For example $(-2)^{-3}=-1/8$ but $(-2)^{-2}=1/4$. Parentheses still determine the base, as in L21.

The PDF rules, for nonzero bases and integer exponents m and n:

| Rule | Worked example |
| --- | --- |
| $a^ma^n=a^{m+n}$ | $2^3\cdot2^2=2^{3+2}=32$ |
| $(a^m)^n=a^{mn}$ | $(2^3)^2=2^3\cdot2^3=2^6=64$ |
| $(ab)^n=a^nb^n$ | $(2\cdot3)^2=2^2\cdot3^2=36$ |
| $a^0=1$ | $7^0=1$ |
| $a^{-n}=1/a^n$ | $2^{-3}=1/8$ |

For a strictly positive integer exponent, the product of bases may also contain zero: $(a^2b)^3=a^6b^3$ for all real a and b. We do not use $0^0$ or negative powers of zero. A power does not distribute over addition: revisit L22.

<span id="module-roots"></span>

**C — Roots and domain (PDF, page 14).**

The PDF defines $\sqrt t$ for $t\ge0$ as the unique nonnegative real number whose square is t. Do not confuse calculating a root and solving an equation:

| Question | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| $\sqrt{25}$ | $5$ |
| $z^2=25$ | $z=-5$ or $z=5$ |
| $\sqrt{-25}$ | Undefined over the reals |

For every real x, $\sqrt{x^2}=|x|$. If x is negative, its opposite is positive. Example: $\sqrt{(-6)^2}=\sqrt{36}=6=|-6|$. The product rule $\sqrt{ab}=\sqrt a\sqrt b$ is used for $a,b\ge0$. It lets us extract a perfect square: $\sqrt{72}=\sqrt{36\cdot2}=6\sqrt2$.

It does not work for addition: $\sqrt{9+16}=5$ but $\sqrt9+\sqrt{16}=7$.

**Simplify without losing the domain.**

The domain is the set of allowed inputs. Before simplifying, write the original conditions.

$$\frac{x^2-9}{x-3}=\frac{(x-3)(x+3)}{x-3}=x+3\quad(x\ne3).$$

At x = 3, the original expression gives 0/0, which is undefined. The simplified polynomial alone equals 6, but this value must never be restored to the original domain.

| Input | $\sqrt x$ | $\sqrt{x^2}$ | $x^{-1}$ | $\frac{x^2-9}{x-3}$ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| −2 | Undefined | 2 | −1/2 | 1 |
| 0 | 0 | 0 | Undefined | 3 |
| 3 | $\sqrt3$ | 3 | 1/3 | Undefined |

## 5. Practise — Combine three actions

### 1. Apply

Calculate $2/3-1/4$.

<details>
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use twelfths.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Explained solution</summary>

$2/3-1/4=8/12-3/12=5/12$. Subtract counts of equal-sized shares.

</details>

</div>


<div className="algebra-exercise">

### 2. Combine

Simplify $(x^2-4)/(x-2)$ and state the domain.

<details>
<summary>Hint</summary>

Factorise the numerator before cancelling.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Explained solution</summary>

For $x\ne2$, $(x^2-4)/(x-2)=(x-2)(x+2)/(x-2)=x+2$. Value $2$ stays excluded even though the final formula could accept it.

</details>

</div>


<div className="algebra-exercise">

### 3. Reason

For $x=-3$, compare $\sqrt{x^2}$, $\sqrt x$ and $x^{-2}$.

<details>
<summary>Hint</summary>

“Square then root” differs from “root directly”.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Explained solution</summary>

$\sqrt{x^2}=3$; $\sqrt x$ is undefined over the reals; $x^{-2}=1/9$. The sign of the exponent does not determine the sign of the result.

</details>

</div>

## 6. Remember — Printable revision sheet

<div className="algebra-revision">

- Fractions: $\frac ab\pm\frac cd=\frac{ad\pm bc}{bd}$ ; $\frac ab\cdot\frac cd=\frac{ac}{bd}$ ; $\frac ab\div\frac cd=\frac{ad}{bc}$.
- Conditions: $b,d\ne0$ ; for division, also $c\ne0$.
- Powers: nonzero bases, integer exponents. $a^ma^n=a^{m+n}$ ; $(a^m)^n=a^{mn}$ ; $(ab)^n=a^nb^n$ ; $a^0=1$ ; $a^{-n}=1/a^n$.
- Roots: $\sqrt t\ge0$ if $t\ge0$ ; $\sqrt{x^2}=|x|$ for every real x. $\sqrt{ab}=\sqrt a\sqrt b$ if $a,b\ge0$.
- Domain before cancellation: nonzero denominator, nonnegative radicand, nonzero base for negative powers. Cancel only common nonzero factors and preserve exclusions.

**Self-check.** Redo an addition, explain a negative exponent, distinguish a root from an equation, then state the domain of a quotient before cancelling. Revisit the relevant module if a step remains difficult.
