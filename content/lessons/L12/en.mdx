# Negating quantified statements


## 1. Understand — Ask the right question

### Your objective

By the end of this lesson, you can negate $\forall x\ P(x)$ and $\exists x\ P(x)$, state negations in everyday language, and use counterexamples and witnesses.

**Prerequisites:** L11 (predicates and quantifiers).


### Diagnostic — negate an inequality

What is the negation of $x>3$? Is it $x<3$ or $x\le3$? Test $x=3$.

<details className="sets-diagnostic">
<summary>Check prerequisites</summary>

It is $x\le3$. Include equality: at 3, the original is false and its negation is true. Revisit [L04](/en/lessons/L04/) and [L11](/en/lessons/L11/).

</details>

To refute “all tickets are valid”, find one invalid ticket; you do not need every ticket to be invalid.

## 2. Visualise — Follow the objects

![Negating the whole sentence swaps for all and exists, then negates the condition.](/diagrams/sets-learning/l12-en.svg)

### Negating “for all”

“In the library, every book is available” fails as soon as one book is not available. Its negation is “In the library, some book is not available”.

### Negating “there exists”

“At the club, some member is under 18” fails when nobody is under 18. Its negation is “At the club, every member is 18 or older”.

## 3. Calculate — Work through and justify

### The two laws

If $P(x)$ is a predicate on $X$:
$$
\neg \forall x\ P(x) \equiv \exists x\ \neg P(x),
$$
$$
\neg \exists x\ P(x) \equiv \forall x\ \neg P(x).
$$

The negation swaps the quantifier and negates the predicate.

### Guided example: natural numbers above 2

Take the domain $\mathbb{N}$, with $0 \in \mathbb{N}$, and $P(n)$ be $n > 2$.

1. $\forall n\ P(n)$ is false: $n = 0$ is a counterexample.
2. Its negation is $\exists n\ \neg P(n)$, which says $\exists n\ n \leq 2$.
3. This is true, and $n = 0$ is a witness.

### Trap: negating only the predicate

$\neg \forall x\ P(x)$ is not $\forall x\ \neg P(x)$. On the domain $\{1, 2\}$ with $P(x)$ being $x > 1$, the first is true because $P(1)$ fails, while the second is false because $P(2)$ holds. “Not every bird flies” does not mean “no bird flies”.

### Negate the whole sentence, even on an empty domain

$\neg$ means NOT and here applies to the whole quantified statement. The symbol $\equiv$ says that both formulations have the same truth in every interpretation of the domain and predicate; this extends the formula comparison from L04 to quantified statements.

If $X$ is empty, “every element satisfies P” is true because no counterexample exists; “some element satisfies P” is false because no witness exists. Their negations are false and true respectively: both laws still work. In the first example, say explicitly “there exists at least one unavailable book”; this does not mean exactly one book.


### Move NOT inwards, one layer at a time

On $X=\{-1,0,2,5\}$, negate $\forall x\in X\ [(x>0)\Rightarrow(x<4)]$. The notation $x\in X$ specifies the domain being scanned; its logical expansion comes in L16.

1. Bracket the whole statement: $\neg[\forall x\in X\ ((x>0)\Rightarrow(x<4))]$.
2. Exchange the quantifier while keeping the domain: $\exists x\in X\ \neg[(x>0)\Rightarrow(x<4)]$.
3. Use $\neg(A\Rightarrow B)\equiv A\land\neg B$: $\exists x\in X\ [(x>0)\land(x\ge4)]$.
4. Check: 5 is positive but not less than 4. It witnesses the negation and refutes the original.

**Complete:** negate $\exists x\in\mathbb R\ [(x<0)\land(x^2>9)]$. Exchange the quantifier, then use De Morgan inside the brackets.

<details className="sets-correction">
<summary>Complete and justify</summary>

$\forall x\in\mathbb R\ [(x\ge0)\lor(x^2\le9)]$. The domain is unchanged and each negation has reached its inequality. The original is true with $x=-4$; its negation is therefore false.

</details>

<details className="sets-extension">
<summary>Go further — Several quantifiers</summary>

Negating $\forall x\in X\ \exists y\in Y\ P(x,y)$ gives $\exists x\in X\ \forall y\in Y\ \neg P(x,y)$. Exchange every quantifier **without changing their order**, then negate the property. Evaluating truth is a separate step. See [L16](/en/lessons/L16/).

</details>

## 5. Practise — Answer without the model

### Two paper questions

Write a justification before opening the solutions. These new examples are not graded or saved.

1. Negate $\forall x\in\mathbb R\ [(x^2>9)\Rightarrow(x>3)]$, leaving no NOT before a quantifier.
2. On $X=\{0,1,2\}$, negate “there exists x such that x is odd”. Decide the original and negated truth values.

<details className="sets-correction">
<summary>Explained solutions</summary>

1. $\exists x\in\mathbb R\ [(x^2>9)\land(x\le3)]$. The original is false and its negation true: witness $x=-4$.
2. “Every x in X is even.” The original is true with witness 1; its negation is false because of 1.

</details>

## 6. Remember — Revision sheet

### Takeaway

To negate a quantified statement, swap $\forall$ and $\exists$ and negate the predicate. A counterexample refutes a “for all”; a witness proves a “there exists”.

Original project text and examples. Coverage follows PDF pages 49–51 in the repository’s course map; no professor-authored text is reproduced.

**Method and traps :** Bracket → exchange each quantifier → keep domains and order → negate the predicate using Boolean laws → check boundaries. $\neg(A\Rightarrow B)\equiv A\land\neg B$.

**Formulas to retrieve:**

$$\neg(\forall x\ P(x))\equiv\exists x\ \neg P(x),\qquad\neg(\exists x\ P(x))\equiv\forall x\ \neg P(x).$$

**Boundaries:** NOT(x > 3) includes 3; NOT(x ≥ 3) excludes it.
