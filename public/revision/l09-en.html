L09 — Sets and Russell’s paradox

Understand • Reason • Justify

1. Membership

∈: element; ⊆: subset. A set can be an element of another set.

2. Russell: proof by contradiction

E = {x | x ∉ x}

Assume E exists as a set; apply its rule to x = E.

E ∈ E ⇒ E ∉ E
E ∉ E ⇒ E ∈ E

Both cases contradict their assumptions. Reject E’s existence as a set, not sets in general.

3. Restricted construction

X = {1, 2, 3, 4} → even? → {2, 4}

Start from a known set: {x ∈ X | P(x)}. Four course families: number sets, enumeration, restricted comprehension, constructions from the preceding sets.

4. Your turn — without looking at the lesson

Explain why one case is not enough for the paradox.

Enumerate {x ∈ {0, 1, 2, 3, 4} | x > 2}. Justify.
