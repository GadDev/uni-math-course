## Mathematical self-check

Initial alphabets are explicit and ordered. Codes start at 1, additions are consecutive. Encoder steps preserve the pre-decision state, do not emit on a dictionary hit, and emit the last phrase without a final addition. Decoder steps complete the preceding pending entry using only the first letter of the newly decoded chunk. The special rule applies only to the next free code with a nonempty previous chunk. Pending entries are displayed separately; the last one is never inserted as a completed entry.

Checked the supplied examples in both directions, including all dictionary additions:

| Message | Initial alphabet | Expected codes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| BANANABANANA | A=1, B=2, N=3 | 2 1 3 5 1 4 6 6 |
| LALALALALA | A=1, L=2 | 2 1 3 5 4 1 |
| ABABABA | A=1, B=2 | 1 2 3 5 |
| ANANAS | A=1, N=2, S=3 | 1 2 4 1 3 |

Additional unary case: AAAAAA with A=1 gives 1 2 3, with additions AA=2 and AAA=3. All 2,046 binary messages of lengths 1–10 were compared against an independently structured greedy encoder, then round-tripped. Generated challenges were checked at multiple seeds including unsigned-integer boundaries; advanced exercises include a deliberate special-case prefix. Invalid first codes, skipped dictionary indices, malformed input and input limits are rejected.

Cost examples are educational comparisons with explicit fixed code widths and no format overhead. For BANANABANANA, 8 codes × 4 bits = 32 bits versus 12 letters × 2 bits = 24 bits. For 40 letters, 18 codes × 5 bits = 90 bits versus 80 bits; strict improvement allows at most 15 such codes. The slides' 12-bit/4096-entry remark is qualified for classroom one-based indices: if zero is unused, 12 bits represent 1–4095. No GIF/TIFF bitstream implementation is claimed.

## Workshop conventions and limits

The alphabet accepts 1–8 distinct uppercase A–Z letters in user-specified order. Messages and decoded output contain 1–96 letters. Custom code sequences accept positive integer indices separated by spaces, commas or semicolons. The pedagogical sandbox has no resets, saturation, reserved control codes, bit packing or persistent learner records. Random exercises are fully determined by the displayed seed and difficulty; decoding exercises are generated by encoding valid messages first.

Exploration supports reversible snapshots. Guided practice checks the output and dictionary addition, preserves explanatory feedback until the learner advances, and allows retries and hints. Exam practice hides evolving state until submission or explicit reveal; automatic marking checks the final result and asks the learner to compare the dictionary and reasoning. Achievements are optional completion messages, not accuracy or mastery claims. Animation respects reduced motion; all controls are native keyboard-accessible elements.
